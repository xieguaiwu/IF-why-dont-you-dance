## What is licensed, and what is not

This repository contains two different things, and they are not under the same
terms.

**The code is MIT.** The Inform 7 source in `Why don't you dance.inform/Source/`
is an original program. So are the build scripts and the test scripts. Those are
released under the MIT licence in `LICENSE`.

**The story is not mine to license.** "Why Don't You Dance?" is a short story by
Raymond Carver, first published in 1981 and collected in *What We Talk About When
We Talk About Love*. Copyright rests with the Carver estate. This game is an
adaptation of it: the characters, the situation, the sequence of events and the
ending all come from Carver. Nothing here grants anyone a right to the story.

So the MIT licence on the code cannot, and does not, make the game itself free to
copy, sell or build on. If you want to do any of those things with the work as a
whole, that is a question for the rights holder.

## What the adaptation actually takes

Measured against the source text, 189 words appear verbatim, in runs of six words
or more, out of about 9,800 words of game text — roughly 2%. Those are lines of
dialogue quoted directly, chiefly:

- the girl's speech in Act Five ("The guy was about middle-aged...")
- the bargaining over the bed and the television
- "They thought they'd seen everything over here. But they haven't seen this"
- "Why don't you dance?"

Everything else — the five-act structure, the point-of-view switches, the puzzles,
the interiority, the descriptions of objects — is written for this game.
